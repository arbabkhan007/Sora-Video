Welcome to your Hamish style Highland cow crochet companion tutorial. Keep your purchased PDF open beside you, because it contains the exact round by round stitch counts. In this video, we’ll walk through the process customers usually need help with: supplies, shaping, assembly, and getting that fluffy Highland cow finish.

Start by gathering worsted weight yarn in ginger brown and tan, a four millimeter crochet hook or the hook size listed in your pattern, a yarn needle, stitch markers, polyester stuffing, twelve millimeter safety eyes, scissors, pins, and a soft wire slicker brush. If your tension is loose, go down a hook size so the stuffing does not show through.

Most of the cow is made in continuous amigurumi rounds. Begin each piece with the starting method your PDF uses, then place a stitch marker in the first stitch of every round. Work single crochet stitches evenly, use increases to widen the shape, and invisible decreases to close it neatly. Count often. If a round feels off, it is faster to fix one round now than to adjust the whole cow later.

Crochet the main body and head first, then make the smaller pieces: muzzle, ears, horns, legs, and tail. Stuff the body and head firmly enough to hold shape, but not so tightly that the stitches stretch. For small pieces like horns and ears, use just a little stuffing, or leave them flat if your PDF instructs that.

Before sewing, pin every part in place. Check the cow from the front, side, and top. The muzzle should sit centered, the eyes should be even, and the legs should allow the cow to sit or stand the way you want. Sew with the same color yarn whenever possible, taking small stitches through both layers so the joins are secure and tidy.

For the signature Highland cow coat, gently brush the brown yarn after the body is assembled. Use light strokes and build the fuzz slowly. Do not brush safety eyes, the muzzle, or delicate details too aggressively. Trim any extra-long fibers with scissors, then shape the fringe around the face.

Finish by weaving in every yarn tail, checking that all parts are firmly attached, and giving the cow a final gentle shaping with your hands. Your handmade Highland cow is ready to photograph, gift, or list in your Etsy shop if your pattern license allows finished item sales. Great job, and enjoy your cozy crochet project.
